Everything you need before you're on the clock

The Seahawks are champions, Mahomes is coming off a torn ACL, A.J. Brown is a Patriot, and the top of the draft board is a two-man argument between Detroit and Atlanta. Four position files below — each one holds every move, tier, name, rookie, sleeper and fade that touches it.

Context

The state of play

Every draft is really an argument about last season. Start with what actually happened, because most of the 2026 board is priced off it.

Seattle won Super Bowl LX on 8 February, beating New England 29–13 behind a defense that sacked Drake Maye six times and forced three turnovers. Running back Kenneth Walker III took MVP — the first back to do it since Terrell Davis — and then promptly left in free agency for Kansas City. Jaxon Smith-Njigba came out of that season as Offensive Player of the Year.

On the fantasy ledger, Christian McCaffrey was the highest-scoring player in the game on a genuinely absurd workload: 403 touches, including his first 300-carry season (311). Josh Allen led quarterbacks at 374.6 points. And the season's biggest league-winner relative to cost was Trevor Lawrence — a useful reminder that the player who decides your title is usually someone you got in the last five rounds.

Three structural facts shape this year's board:

  • Running back is expensive again. There are roughly a dozen legitimate workhorses and ADP has pushed them up, which squeezes the classic Zero-RB build harder than it's been squeezed in years.
  • The rookie class is skill-position thin outside a small handful of names — which is exactly why the veteran backs got repriced upward.
  • Two of the sport's biggest names are injury questions. Patrick Mahomes tore his ACL and LCL in Week 15; Malik Nabers is on his second procedure since a complicated ACL rehab. Both are draftable. Neither is safe.

Approach

How to draft, before we get to who

Most draft advice is a strategy looking for a season. The better habit is a handful of principles that survive whatever actually falls to you at pick 4.7.

Draft tiers, not ranks

The difference between your No. 14 receiver and your No. 15 receiver is noise. The difference between the last man in a tier and the first man in the next one is real. Build your board as blocks and let the board tell you when to move: if a tier has one name left and the next tier is five deep, take the last man standing. If a tier still has six names, take the scarce position instead and come back. This single habit fixes most of the errors that cost people leagues.

The running back question, specifically

Zero RB has been the smart-money build for several seasons and it is genuinely less appealing this year. Two things changed: there are about a dozen true workhorses, and the incoming rookie class is thin at the position, so there's no cheap injury-replacement pipeline waiting in August. Fading the position entirely means betting on a waiver market that may not exist. That doesn't make Zero RB dead — it makes it situational. Use it when the room drafts backs irrationally early and elite receivers and tight ends fall to you. Don't pre-commit to it.

The default this year

Hero RB. Take one workhorse in the first two rounds, hammer receiver through the middle, then attack the backfield again from Round 6 onward where the standalone-value backs live. It gives you the RB1 anchor Zero RB lacks and the receiver depth Robust RB gives up.

Format adjustments

  • Best ball: finish with 6–7 running backs, because you have no waiver wire and injuries are absolute. Correlate — pair a quarterback with one or two of his own pass-catchers. And if you go Zero RB here, an elite tight end is close to non-negotiable; it frees the roster spot you'd otherwise burn on tight end lottery tickets.
  • Auction: nominate players you don't want early, while everyone still has money. Budget 60–65% of your cap on your top four roster spots and refuse to spend more than $2 on quarterback, defense and kicker combined. Be the second-highest bidder on stars and the highest bidder on the tier breaks nobody's watching.
  • Scoring: half-PPR versus full PPR moves pass-catching backs a full round. Know which one you're in before you build your tiers, not after.
RB

Running back

Expensive, thin behind the top twelve, and the position that decides where your draft starts. The 1.01 is a two-man argument.

Draft priority — highest 5 tiers · 2 rookies · 7 gems

The read

Jahmyr Gibbs has averaged 364.5 fantasy points (21.4 per game) over the past two seasons, finishing RB1 and RB3 — and David Montgomery is no longer in Detroit, which points at the largest workload of his career. Detroit also draws the easiest fantasy schedule for running backs this year. Bijan Robinson has finished RB3 and RB2 in the same span and, critically, has never missed a game. Gibbs is the ceiling pick; Bijan is the floor pick. They're the only two backs to finish top-four in each of the last two seasons.

Christian McCaffrey is the third name and the hardest call in the first round. He was the highest-scoring fantasy player alive last season — on 403 touches. Every projection system loves him and every risk model flinches. He is a defensible pick and a defensible fade, which is exactly what a top-five ADP with 300+ carries behind it should look like.

Below the top twelve the position gets thin fast, and because the rookie class didn't restock it, the August waiver wire will not bail you out. That's the whole argument for taking one anchor early.

What changed

Kenneth Walker III SEA → KC · free agency

The Super Bowl MVP left after Seattle declined the franchise tag. Kansas City is a better passing situation than a running one, so his value now rides directly on how much of Mahomes returns.

Travis Etienne Jr. JAX → NO · 4 yrs, $52M

Jacksonville's backfield is now Bhayshul Tuten's — the single most exploitable price on the board. New Orleans gets a real lead back for a suddenly real offense.

Kenneth Gainwell PIT → TB · free agency

Coming off a career-high 1,023 scrimmage yards, Gainwell lands as the 1B to Bucky Irving's 1A on a team that has always fed its backs in the passing game. Going around pick 103.

Zach Charbonnet SEA · PUP list, 22 July

Torn ACL in the playoffs against San Francisco, no set return timeline. With Walker gone, this hands the Seattle job to first-round rookie Jadarian Price to open the year.

Chris Rodriguez WAS → JAX · free agency

Out-earned Rachaad White on the open market and joined the backfield Etienne vacated. Not enough to unseat Tuten, but enough that the split is worth watching in August.

WR

Wide receiver

Unusually stable at the top, which means the money is made in Rounds 4 through 9 — where four separate teams just handed a vacated target tree to somebody.

Draft priority — high 5 tiers · 5 rookies · 6 gems

The read

Ja'Marr Chase remains the gold standard — top-five in receiver points per game in four of his five seasons, and a healthy Joe Burrow gives him as good a claim to the overall WR1 finish as anyone. Puka Nacua and Jaxon Smith-Njigba are the unquestioned focal points of their offenses, with Smith-Njigba arriving as reigning Offensive Player of the Year off a championship. Amon-Ra St. Brown centers one of the league's most productive offenses. All four belong in the first five receivers off the board.

The second tier is about volume that survives circumstance. CeeDee Lamb and Justin Jefferson are the safest bets at the position for the same boring reason: elite target volume travels regardless of who's under center. Jefferson was the WR2 last season at 18.7 PPR points per game with Sam Darnold throwing — quarterback anxiety has been a bad reason to fade him for five straight years.

Then the interesting part. Six offenses lost a primary receiver this offseason, and the players inheriting that volume are being priced on last year's role rather than this year's. That is the exploitable middle of this draft.

What changed

A.J. Brown PHI → NE · for a 2028 1st + 2027 5th

The headline trade of the offseason, reuniting Brown with Mike Vrabel and giving Drake Maye a genuine alpha. The bigger fantasy consequence is in Philadelphia, where DeVonta Smith inherits a receiver room with almost no competition.

D.J. Moore CHI → BUF · Moore + a 5th for a 2nd

Buffalo finally bought Josh Allen a proven perimeter receiver. Chicago condenses to three mouths — Rome Odunze, Luther Burden III, Colston Loveland — the cleanest projection change of the offseason.

Jaylen Waddle MIA → DEN · for a 1st + 3rd, swap of 4ths

Moves from an offense that ranked 29th in neutral passing rate to one that ranked 4th. Becomes a real WR2 rather than a boom-bust flex — though Courtland Sutton remains the top dog in Denver, which caps the ceiling.

Mike Evans TB → SF · 3 yrs, up to $60.4M

Twelve years in Tampa, over. Evans joins a 49ers room that also added Christian Kirk, alongside Ricky Pearsall. Downstream: Emeka Egbuka becomes the unambiguous No. 1 in Tampa Bay.

Michael Pittman Jr. IND → PIT · 3 yrs, $59M

Pittman gets paid in Pittsburgh. In Indianapolis, Josh Downs is the direct beneficiary and belongs in the WR2 conversation with a healthy Daniel Jones throwing.

The rule that makes this work

You cannot roster all of these. Pick two or three across the whole draft, weighted toward the ones whose mechanism is already true rather than the ones that need an injury. Then be ruthless — a deep sleeper who isn't playing by Week 3 is a roster spot, not a strategy.

TE

Tight end

Pay up in Round 2 or wait until Round 11. The middle of this position is the worst value on the entire board.

Draft priority — barbell 4 tiers · 0 rookies · 7 gems

The read

This is the position where the industry genuinely can't agree, and that disagreement is worth more to you than a fake consensus. PFF has Trey McBride and Brock Bowers as the top two. CBS puts Bowers first, expecting him to lead the position in targets. Sports Illustrated ranks McBride first with Tyler Warren second and Bowers third. NFL.com's Dan Parr goes Bowers, then Colston Loveland, then McBride. When four credible shops produce four different orders, the honest read is: these are all the same pick, take whichever falls.

The case for Bowers is situational — Klint Kubiak arriving from Seattle's championship staff, a veteran quarterback to open the year, and a thin receiver room around him. He saw 150-plus targets last year and has a real shot at McBride's single-season tight end reception record (126). The case for McBride is that he already was the No. 1 fantasy tight end last season.

Both cost a second-round pick. That is a lot, and the alternative is real: skip the position entirely until Round 11 and take two shots from a genuinely deep late tier. What you should not do is spend Rounds 5 through 8 here.

Quarterback

Twelve quarterbacks will finish as usable weekly starters. There are twelve teams in your league. Do the arithmetic and draft accordingly.

Draft priority — lowest 4 tiers · 2 rookies · 5 gems

The read

The consensus top of the position runs Josh Allen, Joe Burrow, Drake Maye, Lamar Jackson and Jayden Daniels, with Jaxson Dart, Brock Purdy, Jalen Hurts, Matthew Stafford, Dak Prescott, Caleb Williams and Trevor Lawrence filling out the top twelve. Allen adding D.J. Moore is the single most bullish arrow in the group.

The two bounce-back cases are the interesting ones. Lamar Jackson managed only 17.1 points per game last season and finished QB16 — but that was an injury-wrecked year, he was still QB5 in passing points per attempt, and he is one season removed from being the overall QB1. Jayden Daniels was QB5 in points per game as a rookie (20.9), lost 2025 to injury, and now enters a new system with more under-center work, play-action and pre-snap motion.

None of which should change your plan: take two arms in the last five rounds and stream the better matchup. The value pocket at this position is deep enough that paying up is a choice, not a necessity.

The plan

Your draft template, by slot

The four files above tell you who. This tells you when — and answers the only question that actually matters before your first pick: do I open running back or wide receiver?

The short answer

It depends entirely on where you're sitting, and it depends for a reason you can read straight off the tier boards. There are two Tier-1 running backs and four Tier-1 receivers. That asymmetry decides everything:

The rule

Picks 1–2: open running back. Gibbs and Bijan are a tier of their own and one of them will be there.
Picks 3–6: open wide receiver. Both Tier-1 backs are gone, so an RB here means reaching down a tier while a Tier-1 receiver is still sitting there. Take the better tier.
Picks 7–12: open running back again. The Tier-1 receivers are gone too, and now you're choosing between Tier-2 at both spots — where the running back cliff behind them is steeper than the receiver cliff.

That's the whole logic: never reach across a tier break to fill a position. You open receiver in the middle of Round 1 not because receivers are better, but because at picks 3 through 6 the receivers available are a full tier above the backs available. Six picks later that's reversed.

Slot by slot

Twelve-team snake, PPR. Your first three picks are listed on each card, because the turn is what actually shapes your roster — not the first pick in isolation.

11.01 · 2.12 · 3.01Open RB

Then: back-to-back at the 2/3 turn. Take one Tier-3 RB and one Tier-3 WR — Chase Brown and DeVonta Smith is the dream pair.

The best turn on the board this year. Back-to-back picks let you take a Tier-2 running back and a Tier-2 receiver, or a back and Bowers.

Four pivots for when the board misbehaves

  • 01Four or more backs go in the first six picks. The room is RB-hungry. Take receivers, let the dead zone come to you, and buy the Round 4–6 leverage tier — Tuten, Skattebo, Price — at a discount. This is the one scenario where Zero RB switches on this year.
  • 02Bowers or McBride is there at your Round 2 pick. Take him. It's the only non-RB/WR pick worth making before Round 5, and it means committing to receiver in Rounds 3 and 4 — which is fine, that's where the value is anyway.
  • 03You opened WR-WR. You must take a back with each of your next two picks. Not "best available" — a back. Two receivers and no RB1 is how good drafts become mediocre teams.
  • 04A Tier-1 name falls two rounds past his ADP. Take him, whatever your plan said. Nabers in Round 5 or Rashee Rice in Round 4 are entirely different propositions from Nabers in Round 3 and Rice in Round 2 — price is the whole argument on both.

Before you pick

Draft-morning checklist

  1. 01Re-pull ADP the day of your draft. The numbers on this page are from mid-to-late July and August moves them.
  2. 02Check the injury designation — not the ranking — for every player in your top 40. Mahomes, Nabers, Charbonnet and Rice are the four that move most.
  3. 03Print your tiers, not your ranks. Cross names off as they go and let the tier breaks tell you when to move.
  4. 04Know your scoring cold. Half-PPR versus full PPR moves pass-catching backs a full round.
  5. 05Decide in advance which injury-discount names you're willing to own. Then own no more than one.
  6. 06Take defense and kicker with your last two picks. Not one pick earlier.
