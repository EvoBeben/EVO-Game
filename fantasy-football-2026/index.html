02

Strategy

Principles that survive whatever falls to you at pick 4.7.

  • Draft tiers, not ranks. Move when a tier is about to empty.
  • Hero RB is the default. One anchor early, receivers in the middle, backfields again from Round 6.
  • Zero RB only if the room forces it. Thin rookie class means no waiver bailout.
  • Tight end: Round 2 or Round 11. Nothing in between.
  • Never pay for a quarterback. Two late arms, stream the matchup.
  • D and K with your last two picks. Every year.

Draft plan

RB or WR at pick 1? Answered for all twelve seats.

Two Tier-1 backs. Four Tier-1 receivers. That asymmetry decides it:

Picks 1–2 — open RB. Gibbs or Bijan will be there.

Picks 3–6 — open WR. Both Tier-1 backs are gone; a back here means reaching down a tier while a Tier-1 receiver sits there.

Picks 7–12 — open RB. Tier-1 receivers gone too, and the RB cliff is steeper.

One principle: never reach across a tier break to fill a position.

Best turn on the board. Take a Tier-2 back and a Tier-2 receiver.

  • Four backs go in the first six picks. Take receivers, buy the Round 4–6 leverage tier late. This is when Zero RB switches on.
  • Bowers or McBride at your Round 2 pick. Take him, then receivers in Rounds 3 and 4.
  • You opened WR-WR. Take a back with each of your next two picks. Not "best available" — a back.
  • A Tier-1 name falls two rounds. Take him. Price is the whole argument.
