Front Office & Leadership

  • Poles & Johnson locked in step. GM Ryan Poles' extension aligned him with head coach Ben Johnson — both under contract through the 2029 season, giving Halas Hall its cleanest continuity window in a decade.
  • Coordinators unchanged. Dennis Allen (DC, play-caller) and Declan Doyle (OC) return for Year 2; Richard Hightower stays on special teams. Johnson continues calling the offense.
  • Stadium file still open. President/CEO Kevin Warren reiterated the Arlington Heights project — pitched as zero state construction dollars — while the club keeps doing due diligence on a Hammond, Ind. site, where enabling legislation has already passed. Springfield has signaled the project isn't a 2026 priority.
  • Caleb extension groundwork. Poles can't formally negotiate with Williams until after year three, but the Bears have set the table for a long-term deal next offseason. The green light is Williams' to earn in 2026.

Trades & Major Transactions

  • TradeDJ Moore to Buffalo. Moore plus a 2026 fifth-rounder shipped to the Bills for a 2026 second-round pick. Widely graded a win-win: Buffalo gets Josh Allen a proven separator, Chicago banks a top-64 asset and clears the WR room for its young pass catchers.
  • TradeGarrett Bradbury from New England for a 2027 fifth-rounder — the answer at center after Drew Dalman's abrupt retirement this offseason.
  • SignedDevin Bush, LB — three years, $30M. The tone-setter in a faster, more aggressive front seven.
  • SignedCoby Bryant, S — three years, fresh off Seattle's Super Bowl LX run (15 starts, career-high 4 INTs in 2025).
  • SignedNeville Gallimore, DT (2 yr / $12M), Cam Lewis, CB (2 yr), Kalif Raymond, WR (Johnson/Randle El reunion), Braxton Jones, LT back on a 1-year deal worth up to $10M.
  • Re-signedDL Daniel Hardy (2 yr), LB D'Marco Jackson (2 yr), QB Case Keenum (2 yr), DB Elijah Hicks (1 yr), OL Jordan McFadden (1 yr).
  • CapCole Kmet and Jonah Jackson contracts reworked for space; Kmet now a lock on the 53 with Moore's targets up for grabs.

Player Updates & Camp Battles

  • Caleb Williams, QB. Year 3 after a genuine leap: 3,942 yards, 27 TD, 7 INT, 90.1 rating. Johnson has publicly set a higher completion-percentage bar and praised his offseason work; Williams has also been vocal about wanting grass playing surfaces.
  • Rome Odunze, WR. The stress fracture in his left foot healed without surgery. With Moore gone he's the presumptive WR1 — but Luther Burden III is chasing that title.
  • Colston Loveland, TE. Finished ninth among TEs at 713 yards in a limited rookie role; projection models have him leading Chicago in receiving with 1,000-yard upside.
  • Ozzy Trapilo, OT. Patellar tendon injury in the Wild Card round — expected to open the season on PUP, freeing a roster spot.
  • Left tackle battle. Braxton Jones took every first-team spring rep (44 career starts, healthy) and leads Jedrick Wills Jr. and Theo Benedet for Week 1.
  • Jaylon Johnson, CB. Make-or-break year: missed 10 games in 2025, and his 4-yr/$76M deal has no guarantees left after this season with an out following 2026.
  • Dillon Thieneman, S. No. 25 overall (Oregon), 4.35 forty, ranked the draft's 11th-best pick by ESPN's Matt Miller. Starts opposite Bryant in an all-new safety tandem.
  • Kyle Monangai, RB & Austin Booker, EDGE headline a rising sophomore class expected to absorb real snaps.

The Bottom Line

Chicago spent a cap-constrained offseason raising the floor rather than swinging big: mid-tier defensive money, a traded-for center, and a first-round safety. The offense is now unmistakably Ben Johnson's, built around Williams, Loveland, Odunze and Burden instead of a veteran WR1. The season hinges on whether a rebuilt secondary and a faster front seven can pair last year's league-best takeaway rate with an actual pass rush — and whether Williams' Year 3 earns the extension Poles already has drafted.
